# Doom 3 comparison and roadmap

Status of version 0.12.0 plus the unreleased changes in `docs/CHANGELOG.md`. "Done" items are tested against the bundled test level, and since the unreleased changes also against the free Doom 3 demo's three maps (item 1); the full game's maps are not tested yet.

**Priority**: item 1 now depends mostly on item 5 (triggers and scripts switch the lights on and show the monsters of retail maps), then on the shader compile stalls of item 1. Items 2, 3 and 10 wait for CGE changes (A to J below), most of them proposed as pull requests.

## Done

- PK4 / loose-file virtual file system with override order, `-basepath`, `-game`, `-pk4`.
- Declarations: materials, entityDefs (with `inherit`), model decls (`mesh`, `anim`, `offset`, `skin`, `inherit`, frame commands skipped), skins, sound shaders, tables (indexed).
- Materials: diffuse / bump / specular maps, stage forms, image programs `heightmap`, `addnormals`, others approximated by their source image; alpha test; translucent; two-sided; non-solid; additive / alpha / filter blended stages.
- `.proc` areas, brush models, portals and area BSP (`PointInArea`); `.map` entities.
- Point, projected (spot) and ambient lights with colour, `start_off`.
- md5mesh / md5anim with interpolated playback and skins.
- Static models: LWO2 and ASE (props, `func_static`, light fixtures, items, LWO doors), with skins, collision for `func_*` unless `noclipmodel`.
- Collision models (`.cm`): the world's player-blocking polygons (solid and `playerclip`) collide instead of the render geometry.
- GUIs: `.gui` windowDef trees rendered (software) onto `guisurf` surfaces with the entity's `gui_*` state.
- Material stages: texture matrices (`scroll`, `translate`, `scale`, `centerScale`, `shear`, `rotate`) and animated colors on surfaces; `cameraCubeMap` / `cubeMap` skies as a skybox; every plain stage drawn (overlays, on the same triangles with CGE's `DepthMode`), `deform sprite` billboards; glass with heat haze (`heatHaze*` programs, CGE's `ScreenTexture`) or a cube map reflection, reflections (`texgen reflect`) over lit surfaces (unreleased); other glass as a faint tint.
- Stencil shadows (CGE shadow volumes) for the strongest light at the player, from brush entities, static models, md5 models (big ones through a box per joint) and the level's `.cm` brushes (walls, pillars); `noshadows` materials and lights.
- Light volumes: Doom 3's box / frustum lights without distance attenuation, `light_start` / `light_end`, projection and falloff shapes (analytic stand-ins for the images), `shaderParm4` time offsets.
- Light shaders: animated stage color registers with expressions and tables (strobes, flickers, pulses), `colored`, `shaderParm`s.
- Portal visibility: areas, entities, models and lights hidden when not visible through the portals; doors close portals.
- `func_door` (proximity trigger, `movedir`, `lip`, `speed`, `time`, `wait`, `locked`, `no_touch`, sounds), `func_static`, `func_rotating`.
- Speakers with looping and one-shot sound shaders.
- Player movement, flashlight, pistol, health / armor, death and restart; simple melee monsters.
- Editor component `TDoom3Map`, HUD, console, menu with the map list, autotest harness, parser unit tests, CI.
- Retail data (unreleased): the Doom 3 demo's `demo/` directory; lights and entities in all areas their bounds touch (BSP); monsters' `hide` / `trigger` / skill flags; undefined `textures/common/entitygui*` as GUI screens; `tools/smoke_maps.ps1`; debug console commands `lightsat`, `ent`, `guidump`.

## Classic Doom 3

[Classic Doom 3](https://dhewm3.org/mods.html#classic-doom-3) (Flaming Sheep Software, 2007, version 1.3.1) remakes episode 1 of the original Doom (Knee-Deep in the Dead) in Doom 3. It needs the full Doom 3 data. Its data is a zip from [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/downloads/classic-doom-3-131), extracted into the Doom 3 directory (a `cdoom/` mod directory); dhewm3 starts it with `+set fs_game cdoom`. Its game code was released under the GPL and lives in the [`cdoom` branch of dhewm3-sdk](https://github.com/dhewm/dhewm3-sdk/tree/cdoom) (commit "Import Classic Doom 3"), which tells what this port must do beyond the base game:

- **Mod directory**: `-game cdoom` already mounts `<basepath>/cdoom` over `base`; also accept dhewm3's `+set fs_game cdoom` (and `fs_game_base`), list the mods found next to `base/` in the main menu, and check that the mod's decls override base ones (we keep the first definition of a decl: the mod's pk4s must be searched first).
- **Secrets** (`idSecret` in `Misc.cpp`): an entity that marks a secret area when the player touches it (or when triggered), counted per map, with a "secret found" message.
- **End of level and intermission** (`idTarget_EndLevelCdoom` in `Target.cpp`, `idPlayer::OpenStats` / `StatsProceed`): stops the sounds, shows the statistics GUI (kills, items, secrets and time, as percentages like Doom) and loads the next map, keeping the player's health, armor, weapons and ammo between maps.
- **Doom pickups** (`Item.cpp`, `Player.cpp`): health bonus (`type` 4, up to the player's `maxbonushealth`), armor bonus and mega armor (`type` 5 / 6, up to `maxbonusarmor`), counting picked items (`inv_name` set) for the statistics.
- **Kills counted** for the statistics (`AI.cpp`), **autorun** (`ui_autoRun`), **music speakers** (`s_music` key) with their own volume (`s_music_vol`).
- Its weapons, monsters and level logic are entity defs and Doom 3 scripts: they need the gameplay of item 5 (weapons with view models, items, triggers and movers, the script interpreter and the real AI).
- Multiplayer parts (`cdoomDM`, player skins) are out of scope.

Steps: load its maps like the retail maps (item 1), implement the entities above in `Doom3World`, give `tools/smoke_maps.ps1` a `-Game cdoom` parameter (its map names are to be read from the mod's `maps/`, they are not known here), and check that each map can be finished (the mod's data can't be bundled either).

## Features from dhewm3

[dhewm3](https://github.com/dhewm/dhewm3) is the maintained Doom 3 GPL source port (it doesn't support Doom 3 BFG). Many of its changes are about the C++ code base (64-bit, SDL, CMake, compilers, tools) and come with Castle Game Engine here. These are the ones this port needs too:

| dhewm3 feature | Here |
|---|---|
| Game data: patched 1.3.1 data, Resurrection of Evil (`d3xp`, `fs_game_base`), the free **Doom 3 demo** data | The demo is loaded (`demo/`, item 1); the full game with `-basepath`; Resurrection of Evil is item 8. |

| Widescreen and any resolution; aspect-ratio-independent GUIs (HUD and menus), 4:3 crosshair scaling | Doom 3 GUIs are 640 x 480: render them 4:3 (pillarboxed) or scale by their anchors; CGE UI scaling for our own HUD. |
| Gamepad support (with ducking and jumping on triggers) | CGE's `Controllers` (game controllers) API. |
| OpenAL EFX reverb (EAX-like effects from the maps' `efxs/*.efx` files) | Done: `Doom3Efx` reads `efxs/<map>.efx` and the listener's area number, location or `"default"` chooses the effect, played with CGE's `SoundEngine.Reverb` (CGE change L). Console `reverb`, `s_useEAXReverb`. The demo has no `.efx` files, so only the test level's is checked. |
| Advanced settings menu (graphics, audio, controls, key bindings) | A CGE UI view: resolution, fullscreen, vsync, gamma / brightness, volume, bindings. |
| Gamma and brightness applied in shaders | A CGE screen effect. |

| Savegames, quicksave slots, autosave at level start (`com_disableAutoSaves`) | Save the world state (entities, movers, monsters, player, scripts). |
| `nospecular` light parameter (`allow_nospecular`) | Item 10. |
| MD3 models (used by mods) | A loader like the md5 one. |
| MD5 "shrivel" effect (`SHADERPARM_MD5_SKINSCALE`, creatures burning away) | Done for gibbed bodies (`TD3Md5Instance.SkinScale`, 5g); burn deaths still need shader parms on md5 materials. |
| Mouse: inverted look (`m_invertLook`), High-DPI mice, always run (`in_allowAlwaysRunInSP`) | Options of our player navigation. |

## Modern rendering and features from RBDOOM-3-BFG

[RBDOOM-3-BFG](https://github.com/RobertBeckebans/RBDOOM-3-BFG) (Robert Beckebans and contributors, since 2012) modernizes Doom 3 BFG: rendering closer to Doom 2016, same gameplay. Its renderer features are the reference for a modern mode here. They should stay optional (console variables, like RBDOOM's), so the classic look can still be compared with dhewm3. Its BFG-specific parts (`.bimage` / `.bproc`, Flash GUIs) belong to item 8; its C++ code base changes (CMake, SDL, NVRHI) come with Castle Game Engine here.

| RBDOOM-3-BFG feature | Here | CGE |
|---|---|---|
| **PBR** (GGX Cook-Torrance, roughness / metallic) for new materials; old `specularmap`s also through GGX, roughness estimated from the gloss | Material keywords `basecolormap`, `normalmap`, `rmaomap` (R roughness, G metallic, B ambient occlusion) and `_rmao` overrides of old materials, mapped to `PhysicalMaterial`. glTF's metallic-roughness texture has roughness in G and metallic in B, so the RMAO image needs a channel swizzle when loaded (a `d3img:` image program). Old materials stay Phong unless a mode estimates roughness. | Has PBR (`TPhysicalMaterialNode`). The light volume effects (`Doom3LightEffects`) use `PLUG_light_scale`, which the physical lighting model has too: to test. |
| **HDR and gamma-correct lighting**, ACES filmic tone mapping | Option: physical materials with `ColorSpace = csLinear` and `ToneMapping := tmACES`. Doom 3's light rigs were made for gamma space: RBDOOM tweaks the light formulas with constants; compare with dhewm3 by mean luma. | Has `ToneMapping` (ACES and others) and `ColorSpace`. Rendering goes to an 8-bit framebuffer, so values over 1 are clamped per pass (change I: a floating-point framebuffer). |
| **Baked indirect lighting**: environment probes (`env_probe` entities, or one per BSP area) with L4 spherical harmonics for diffuse light and GGX-convolved mips for specular; irradiance volumes (a light grid per BSP area, octahedral SH per grid point, 8 nearest points interpolated per pixel); `bakeEnvironmentProbes` / `bakeLightGrids`; one prebaked fallback probe (Mars City 1's lobby) | Fixes Doom 3's pitch-black areas. Steps: a fallback environment (`EnvironmentLight` with one baked cube map) for the whole map; per-area probes chosen by the area a shape is in; then light grids. Baking renders 6 views per probe with CGE, convolves them and writes them next to the map (RBDOOM writes EXR under `env/maps/...`; its baked data for the game is on ModDB, format to check). | Has `EnvironmentLight` (image-based lighting with diffuse and specular textures, physical materials). Missing: several probes with a choice per shape, irradiance volumes (change P). |
| **Soft shadow maps** for all light types in a shadow atlas; cascaded shadow maps for parallel (sun) lights | Console `r_shadowMaps 1`: the shadow lights (point lights) cast shadow maps in CGE's atlas instead of shadow volumes (CGE changes B and R). Follow-ups (tile sizes by screen size, cascade blending, casters hidden by portals, cascades per viewport): [mariuz/castle-engine#13](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/13) (merged into `doom3-port`). Next: every light casting, measured on the demo maps. | Point lights (6 views), cascades (`DirectionalLight.shadowCascades`), one shared atlas, global lights casting on all scenes: [mariuz/castle-engine#12](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/12) (changes B and R, in `doom3-port`). |
| **SSAO** that only darkens the indirect light, like Frostbite | Option once there is indirect light (plain SSAO darkens direct light too). | Has `TCastleViewport.ScreenSpaceAmbientOcclusion` (whole image). |
| **Screen-space reflections** (blood, wet floors) | Option for physical materials. | Has `TCastleViewport.ScreenSpaceReflections`. |
| **SMAA and temporal anti-aliasing (TAA)** | TAA: console `r_taa 1` (CGE change Q). SMAA: not yet. | `TCastleViewport.TemporalAntiAliasing` (change Q, [mariuz/castle-engine#11](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/11), in `doom3-port`): jitter, motion vectors from the depth and the camera, neighborhood clamping; MSAA (`TCastleWindow.AntiAliasing`). |
| **Filmic post-processing**: chromatic aberration, blue-noise dithering; retro modes (C64, Amstrad CPC, Sega Genesis, PSX) | Screen effects (GLSL) behind console variables like RBDOOM's `r_useFilmicPostFX` and `r_renderMode`. | Has screen effects (`TCastleScreenEffect`, `ScreenEffect` nodes). |
| **Masked software occlusion culling** (Intel MOC) on top of the BSP portals | Portal visibility already hides most areas; measure first (CPU GL submission dominates, see ARCHITECTURE.md "Performance"). | Has hardware occlusion queries (`OcclusionCulling`); a software rasterizer would be new. |
| **Vulkan / DX12** (NVRHI) | Out of scope: CGE renders with OpenGL / OpenGL ES / WebGL. | |
| Renderer stats with ImGui (`com_showFPS 2`), RenderDoc / Nsight markers | A stats overlay with the `Profile:` numbers; debug groups around the passes for Nsight (see "Performance"). | Has the inspector (F8 in debug builds); `glPushDebugGroup` markers would be a small change. |
| **Modding formats**: glTF 2 `.glb` models (static and skinned), Wavefront OBJ, `.png` / `.exr` / `.hdr` images, `invertGreen( ... )` image program, MikkTSpace tangents | Models in entity defs and `model` keys loaded through CGE's loaders; `invertGreen` with the other image programs. | Loads glTF (with skinning), OBJ, PNG and Radiance `.hdr` (Vampyre); `.exr` loading not found. CGE computes its own tangents: compare with MikkTSpace normal maps. |
| **TrenchBroom** mapping, `rbdmap` compiler with AAS, maps compiled from `.glb` | Out of scope (this port reads compiled maps); the test map generator (`tools/make_test_pk4.py`) stays. | |
| Skippable cinematics, classic flashlight, Bink video (libbinkdec / FFmpeg) | Cinematics are skippable (`skipcinematic`, Escape), the flashlight exists. Doom 3's (not BFG's) videos are RoQ (`.roq`): a decoder to video textures, with the GUI work of item 4. | Has movie textures (image sequences, videos through FFmpeg). |

Order: PBR materials (keywords and `_rmao`), then a fallback environment probe and ACES tone mapping as a "modern" option, then per-area probes and SSAO on the indirect light. The CGE side of probes and light grids (P), soft shadow maps (B, R) and TAA (Q) is merged into `doom3-port`, and the port uses them behind console variables (`r_envProbes`, `r_shadowMaps`, `r_taa`); still to do here: PBR materials, baked light grids per area, every light casting shadow maps.

A, B, C, D, F, J, M, N and O were checked against the CGE source, P, Q and R against CGE master (October 2026); the others come from building this port.

**A. Shadow volumes from several lights.** `TCastleViewport` takes one main light for shadow volumes (`MainLightForShadowVolumes`, the light with `shadowVolumesMain`), and `RenderFromView3D` runs the stencil pass (`RenderShadowVolume`) once for it. Doom 3 shadows every light: one stencil clear + shadow volume pass + additive lit pass per shadow casting light that reaches the view.

Workaround: `TDoom3Map.UpdateShadowLight` picks the strongest light at the player every frame.

Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#740](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/740) (draft) adds `TCastleViewport.ShadowVolumesMaxLights` (default 1 = unchanged):
- the receivers are rendered once with the casting lights off (ambient, emissive, other lights, depth buffer);
- per light: stencil clear, its shadow volumes, then an additive (`one, one`) pass of only that light where the stencil is 0, with shaders built with `CASTLE_LIGHT_CONTRIBUTION_ONLY` (no emissive, ambient or fog color, unlit shapes black);
- an example (`examples/viewport_and_scenes/shadow_volumes_several_lights`), API docs, and a rendering test in CGE's suite.

It was checked with this port's test level (up to 8 lights, Phong materials with light volume effects, no artefacts). Still missing after it:
- a scissor rectangle / depth bounds per light, so each pass only covers the light's screen area, as Doom 3 does (scissor: done, follow-up below);
- the gamma issue (change I);
- the cge-www manual page `shadow_volumes.adoc` still says one light (to update if the PR is accepted);
- here: done (unreleased), CI builds against `doom3-port`: `r_shadowLights N` (item 2).

Follow-up merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#9](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/9)): each light's passes (stencil clear, shadow volumes, additive pass) are limited by a scissor rectangle to where its radius reaches, and lights that reach nothing visible are skipped (`ShadowVolumeLightScissor`; console `r_useScissor 0` here compares). The rendering is the same (checked in CGE's tests, and on the test map: no differences outside moving objects). Depth bounds (`EXT_depth_bounds_test`) are still missing.

**B. Shadow maps for point lights.** `CastleInternalShadowMaps` computes projections only for spot and directional lights (`AutoCalculateProjectionForDirectionalLight`, spot cut-off angles): a point light needs a cube shadow map (6 faces, or a dual paraboloid map) and the shader lookup for it. Also, shadow map receivers are the shapes of the same scene as the light (`ProcessShadowMapsReceivers` per scene); the map's areas and lights are separate scenes here, so receivers in other scenes (or global lights casting into every scene) are needed too. Either A or B gives several shadow casting lights.

Merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#12](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/12), with R, 2026-10-21): a shadow map has views (tiles); a `PointLight` with `shadows TRUE` renders 6 (cube faces, a bit wider than 90 degrees), the shader picks the face by the direction from the light and looks up a bit along the normal (no shadow acne); its range is the farthest point of the world within the light's radius. Global lights with `shadows TRUE` cast on the shapes of all scenes: their scene prepares, updates and releases their shadow maps even without receivers of its own. Here: console `r_shadowMaps 1` (`TDoom3Map.ShadowMaps`, `SetLightCasts` sets `Shadows` instead of `ShadowVolumes` on the shadow lights).

**C. Textures in light source effects.** A sampler declared in an `Effect` on a light (`PLUG_light_scale`) is never bound, because CGE loads textures only for shapes' appearances. Doom 3 lights are a projection image times a falloff image, both textures. Needed: load and bind `ImageTexture` fields of light effects (as for material effects). Workaround: `Doom3LightEffects` uses analytic shapes (round, square, the default falloff), not the light's images.

Cause (found while fixing it): shapes prepare the textures of the lights of their own scene (`TShape.EnumerateTextures` over `State.Lights`), but our lights are global lights in a separate scene (`TDoom3Map.LightScene`, `CastGlobalLights`), so nothing prepared them and `TTextureResources.Bind` failed. Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#741](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/741) (draft): `TCastleScene.PrepareGlobalLightsTextures` prepares the textures of its global lights' effects (and `EnvironmentLight` textures) every frame in `LocalRender`, before frustum culling (a scene with only lights has an empty bounding box), and in `PrepareResources`; example `examples/shaders/shader_effects_light_projection` (renamed from `shader_effects_on_light`, to not be confused with J's `shader_effects_on_lights`: a light projecting an image; the light, its Effect with the images as `sampler2D` fields and the lit objects are X3D files loaded by scenes in the design file), and a test that fails on master. While reworking the example: changing an `ImageTexture`'s `url` in a light effect of another scene doesn't reload it (only shapes using a texture are notified), so the example switches images with an `image_index` uniform; a possible follow-up. Once it is in a CGE release, `CreateLightEffect` can take the light shader's projection image and the `lightFalloffImage` as `SFNode` fields (`sampler2D`), replacing the analytic shapes.

Follow-up, colored light images: `PLUG_light_scale` multiplies the light by a float, so with #741 alone the images are grayscale masks, while many Doom 3 light images are colored. [castle-engine/castle-engine#742](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/742) (draft, stacked on #741, to merge after #740 and #741) adds `PLUG_light_color_scale(inout vec3 color_scale, normal_eye, light_dir)` to the Phong and physical lighting models (the light's contribution is multiplied by `scale * color_scale`), extends the example with a colored stained glass image (a third sampler field) and documents both plugs in the shader's comments, with a test (Phong and physical materials) that fails without the plug. Here, once both are released: `CreateLightEffect` passes the projection image and the `lightFalloffImage` as `sampler2D` fields and the effect uses `PLUG_light_color_scale` with `projection.rgb * falloff.rgb` (Doom 3 multiplies the light color by both).

**D. Depth offset for coplanar layers.** CGE renders with `DepthFunc = less` and uses `glPolygonOffset` only for wireframe. Doom 3 draws every material stage on the same triangles (`depthFunc equal` / `lequal`) and decals with `polygonOffset`. Needed: a per-appearance depth function (`lequal` / `equal`) and polygon offset (factor, units), for example an Appearance field or a `DepthMode` node like InstantReality's (`depthFunc`, `polygonOffset`). Workaround: overlay stages are copies moved 0.25 units along the normals (`OverlayOffset`), which can show at grazing angles. Started: [mariuz/castle-engine#2](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/2) adds a `DepthMode` node in `Appearance.depthMode` (`depthFunc`, `polygonOffset`), with tests and the example `examples/viewport_and_scenes/depth_mode_layers_and_decals`; merged into `doom3-port`. The material stages now use it: drawn with `lequal` on the same triangles (alpha-tested stages also with a polygon offset), `OverlayOffset` is gone. Still to do: the `polygonOffset` material keyword (decals), and proposing it upstream.

**E. Screen and environment textures in materials.** Doom 3 glass and heat haze read the rendered screen (`_currentRender`) and cube maps with `texgen reflect`. Needed: a "screen color" texture available to shader effects of transparent shapes (a copy of the opaque pass), and reflection texture coordinates from cube maps working together with the unlit material. Workaround: `GlassAppearance`, a faint additive tint. Started: [mariuz/castle-engine#3](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/3) adds a `ScreenTexture` node (the colors rendered before the shapes with blending, copied every frame, resolved with `glBlitFramebuffer` when anti-aliasing), with tests and the example `examples/shaders/shader_effects_screen_texture` (glass and heat haze); cube map reflections (`TextureCoordinateGenerator` `WORLDSPACEREFLECTIONVECTOR`) already work with unlit materials (a test keeps them working). Merged into `doom3-port`, with all `ScreenTexture` nodes sharing one texture (copied once per frame, only when a shape used it in the last frame) and tested on OpenGLES 3 (ANGLE: `glCopyTexSubImage2D` failed there, so the blit is used whenever available). Here: glass with a `heatHaze*` stage samples a `ScreenTexture` like Doom 3's programs, `texgen reflect` stages reflect their cube map on glass and over lit surfaces (see ARCHITECTURE.md "Material stages"); `GlassAppearance`'s tint is left for mirrors and videos.

**F. Silhouette edges kept across animation.** CGE finds a caster's manifold edges by comparing every edge with every other one (`TShapeShadowVolumes.CalculateIfNeededManifoldAndBorderEdges`), and again whenever the coordinates change, so an animated (skinned) md5 mesh repeats the quadratic search every frame. The topology (triangle indexes) doesn't change, only positions: CGE could keep the edge pairs (vertex index pairs) while the index list stays the same, and build them with a hash map instead of a linear search. Workaround: md5 meshes over 1500 triangles cast with boxes per joint (`BuildShadowHulls`).

Checked while working on it (CGE master with N): CGE already keeps the edges when only coordinates change (`TShape.Changed` with `ChangedOnlyCoord`), so the edge search isn't the per-frame cost. What is: the caster triangles are created again by `LocalTriangulate`, which generates the whole geometry arrays (normals, texture coordinates) to read the positions, 9.1 ms per frame for a 19 800 triangle mesh; and a bug, a triangle that becomes degenerate is left out when they are created again while the kept edges refer to the triangles by index. [mariuz/castle-engine#4](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/4) (draft, into `doom3-port`) records each triangle's coordinate indexes the first time and then only moves the same triangles (0.4 ms; 14.1 -> 10.1 ms per frame for that mesh animated with shadow volumes), creating them again (and the edges, when the count changed) only for geometry whose arrays aren't ordered like the coordinates. Here, measured with it: md5 meshes casting with their own triangles cost 0.4% of the CPU time in Mars City 2 (a 2261 triangle zombie), and look better than the joint boxes; #4 is merged into `doom3-port` (with the example `examples/viewport_and_scenes/shadow_volumes_animated_caster`; the tests pass on OpenGLES 3 too), and md5 models whose meshes are all closed now cast with their own triangles whatever their size (`r_md5ShadowClosed`); the others keep the joint boxes, since open meshes don't cast at all. In the demo, most monsters' meshes aren't closed (zombies, imps, sentry: joint boxes), the marine's are.

**G. Casters that aren't drawn.** Shadow volumes are rendered only for visible scenes and shapes. Doom 3 has shadow-only geometry (`shadowModel`s in `.proc`, the closed solids of the map). Needed: a scene or shape that casts shadow volumes without being drawn (for example `Appearance.shadowCaster` with a "render" flag, or a `TCastleScene.Visible := False` that still casts). Workaround: the `.cm` brush casters are real black geometry 1 unit inside the walls, and md5 joint boxes are drawn inside the body.

Started: shapes with `visible FALSE` already weren't drawn but still had their shadow volumes rendered, as if they were in the depth buffer (no light cap in z-pass), so shadows seen through the place of the caster were lit. [mariuz/castle-engine#5](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/5) (draft, into `doom3-port`) renders them like transparent casters (with caps, regular depth test), which makes `visible FALSE` shapes proper shadow-only casters, with a test. The castle-doom3 branch `invisible-shadow-casters` makes the brush casters and md5 joint boxes invisible (shadows unchanged, no black boxes poking out of thin bodies), to merge once #5 is in `doom3-port`. Done in the port: the `.cm` brush casters and md5 joint boxes are invisible (merged), and Doom 3's precomputed `shadowModel`s from the `.proc` cast for their light as invisible casters (`r_prelightShadows`). #5 is merged into `doom3-port`, with an example. A whole scene that casts without being drawn isn't needed: invisible shapes in a scene that `Exists` do it.

**H. Light attenuation to zero at the radius.** CGE cuts a light off hard at its `radius` and its attenuation never reaches zero, so a Doom 3 light box needs a radius twice its size and a low attenuation to hide the edge (`BuildLights`). Needed: an optional smooth falloff to zero at the radius (as glTF `KHR_lights_punctual` range).

Started: [mariuz/castle-engine#6](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/6) (draft, into `doom3-port`) adds `radiusFalloff` to positional lights (`TCastlePointLight` / `TCastleSpotLight.RadiusFalloff`): the light is multiplied by glTF's window `clamp(1 - (d/r)^4, 0, 1)^2` (shaders and CPU), and glTF lights with a `range` use it, with tests (also loading a glTF `range`) and the example `examples/viewport_and_scenes/light_radius_falloff`; the tests pass on OpenGLES 3 (ANGLE) too. Merged into `doom3-port`. Here: with `r_lightVolumes 0`, point lights have the light box's own radius with `RadiusFalloff` (and spot lights fade at the end of their frustum) instead of a doubled radius; at the Mars City Underground elevator that brings the mean luma from 19.9 / 23.6 to 22.4 / 26.7 (dhewm3 25.7 / 27.4, light volumes 23.4 / 27.1). The flashlight and the muzzle flash fade too. Light volumes (the default) compute Doom 3's falloff in their effect, so they don't need it.

**I. Light passes added in linear color space.** The additive passes of A blend in the color buffer after the shader has applied gamma correction or tone mapping, and gamma(a) + gamma(b) > gamma(a + b): shapes rendered with gamma correction (`ColorSpace = csLinear`, or the default `csLinearWhenPhysicalMaterial` with PBR materials) come out too bright where several lights overlap. Needed: render the shadowed passes to a linear framebuffer (sRGB framebuffer with `GL_FRAMEBUFFER_SRGB`, or a floating point one) and convert once at the end. This port uses Phong materials without gamma correction, so A is exact here. Merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#7](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/7)): `TCastleViewport.HighDynamicRange` (a float color buffer in linear colors, converted once); console `r_highDynamicRange 1` turns it on here. Checked on the test map: opaque surfaces and shadows look the same (difference within the run-to-run noise), but blended stages come out darker, since blending also happens in linear colors and Doom 3 blends in screen colors (the green glass pane view 18% darker, the pillar's additive reflection 6%). So the port keeps it off; it only matters if the port moves to physical materials or tone mapping.

Started: [mariuz/castle-engine#7](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/7) (draft, into `doom3-port`, based on A's branch) adds `TCastleViewport.HighDynamicRange`: the world is rendered to a `GL_RGBA16F` color buffer (through the screen effects texture), shapes output linear colors, and gamma correction and tone mapping are applied once, before the viewport's children and screen effects. In its test, two overlapping lights on a physical floor measure 0.28 in one pass, 0.41 as two light passes, and 0.28 as two light passes with `HighDynamicRange`. Next: an example, OpenGLES; here it only matters if the port moves to physical materials or tone mapping (RBDOOM-3-BFG style HDR).

**J. Light effects in the shader code hash (a CGE bug).** Effects on materials, groups and textures are added to the shader program's hash by node reference (`TShaderCodeHash.AddEffects`), because effects with the same code can have different uniform values, set when the program is linked. Effects on lights are not (`TLightShader.Prepare`): shapes lit by different lights of the same type share one program, with the first shape's light effects and their uniforms. With this port's per-light volume effects, most shapes of a retail map used other lights' volumes. The fix adds `Hash.AddEffects(Node.FdEffects)` in `TLightShader.Prepare`: [castle-engine/castle-engine#743](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/743), merged into CGE master on 2026-10-13 (Michalis Kamburelis reworked the example: renamed `shader_effects_on_lights`, setup in the design file), with a test that fails without it and the example `examples/shaders/shader_effects_on_lights` (three lamps, same effect code, different uniforms; before the fix two tiles show the first lamp's rings); with it Mars City 1's start gets a little brighter (mean luma 11.4 -> 13.7). It means more shader programs (one per set of light effects), so the shader compile item above matters more.

**K. Per-light data in a shared light effect.** A light effect's uniforms are fields of its `Effect` node, so lights that need different values (every Doom 3 light has its own volume matrix) need their own `Effect` nodes, and since J every set of light effect nodes on a shape is its own shader program. Doom 3 levels have hundreds of lights: hundreds of programs, compiled when an area first comes into view. Needed: a way for one `Effect` node to be shared by many lights while reading values per light, for example custom fields on the light node passed as `<Light>`-indexed uniforms (`castle_LightSource<Light>...` like the built-in ones), with the light number substituted in the effect code. Merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#8](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/8)): light nodes accept custom fields, set per shape as uniforms `<field name><light number>`, and `<Light>` in a light's effect code becomes the light number. The port uses it when the CGE it is built with supports it (`SharedLightEffectsAvailable`): lights of the same shape (point / projected, with or without a projection) share one `Effect`, each with its own `d3_light_matrix_` field (the test map: 10 lights share 3 effects). In a controlled test 8 lights with an effect each made 8 shader programs, sharing made 1. Without it, the old effect per light.

**L. Reverb in the sound engine's public API.** The full game has `efxs/*.efx` files with EAX reverb settings for its maps (not in the demo data; the format is checked against dhewm3's reader and the test level's file), which dhewm3 plays with OpenAL EFX.

OpenAL itself supports this. EFX (`ALC_EXT_EFX`: effects, filters, auxiliary effect slots, reverb and EAX reverb) is an OpenAL 1.1 extension, implemented by Creative's OpenAL and by OpenAL Soft: OpenAL Soft's ChangeLog (it starts at 1.16.0) already treats EFX and the EAXReverb effect as existing there, adds more effects in 1.16 / 1.19, has 16 auxiliary sends per source in 1.18 (6 since 1.21), and the legacy EAX extensions in 1.22. Checked on this machine with console `soundinfo`: the OpenAL bundled next to the exe (Creative's router `OpenAL32.dll` and `wrap_oal.dll`, device "Generic Software", OpenAL 1.1) has EFX 1.0 with 1 auxiliary send per source, and creates an EAX reverb effect in an auxiliary effect slot without errors.

CGE also has the bindings: `CastleInternalEFX` (the EFX API and Creative's EAX reverb constants) and `Load_EFX`, and its OpenAL backend checks EFX when it opens the device (`TOpenALSoundEngineBackend.EFXSupported`). What's missing is the public API: the backend class is private to `CastleOpenALSoundBackend`, and an OpenAL source's auxiliary send (`alSource3i(Source, AL_AUXILIARY_SEND_FILTER, Slot, 0, Filter)`, needed for every playing sound that should reverberate) can't be reached from `TCastleSound` / `TCastlePlayingSound`. The unit's comment says it is "NOT yet available through the public Castle Game Engine API" and points to an `efx_demo` example that is no longer in the engine.

Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#747](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/747) adds `SoundEngine.Reverb` (`TCastleSoundReverb`: `Enabled` and the EFX EAX reverb parameters, with EFX's defaults and ranges) and `SoundEngine.ReverbSupported`. The OpenAL backend keeps one auxiliary effect slot with an EAX reverb (standard reverb as a fallback), and spatial sources send to it. It comes with tests (`TestReverbParameters`, `TestReverbBackend`) and the example `examples/audio/sound_reverb` (environments from a small room to a hangar). It is merged into `doom3-port`, and the port uses it: `Doom3Efx` parses `.efx`, `TViewPlay.UpdateReverb` chooses the effect per area and location of the listener like dhewm3 and assigns it to `SoundEngine.Reverb` (nothing happens when EFX isn't available: `ReverbSupported` is false). Checked with OpenAL Soft on Linux (EFX 1.0, EAX reverb) in the test level: room A gets `room_a` by its location, room B `"1"` by its area number. Differences from dhewm3: only spatial sounds reverberate (dhewm3 sends all sounds, the player's too), and there is no `s_alReverbGain` (the effect slot's gain).

Possible follow-ups (not in #747):

- reverb for the FMOD and WebAudio backends;
- several reverb zones at once;
- a way for individual 3D sounds to opt out;
- other EFX effects such as echo, chorus and filters.

**M. Shadow volume data prepared only for scenes that cast shadows.** `TCastleSceneCore.PrepareResources` with `prShadowVolume` (which the viewport passes for every scene when it renders shadow volumes) calculated the manifold and border edges of every shape, also in scenes with `CastShadows = False`, which never render shadow volumes. (Shapes with `shadowCaster FALSE` in casting scenes were already cheap: they have no caster triangles.) For a map's big open area meshes this was the biggest part of the first frame (Mars City Underground: about 5 s of 21). Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#745](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/745), which prepares shadow volumes only when `CastShadows` is true (edges are still calculated lazily if it changes later), with `TTestScene.TestPrepareShadowVolumesOnlyCasters` (fails without the fix) and the example `examples/viewport_and_scenes/shadow_volumes_non_casting_scene` (a 20 000 triangle level of separate tiles: 2.2 s before, 0.03 s after; 45 000 triangles: 14.4 s before, 0.02 s after). Workaround until then: `TD3Scene` (`Doom3Scene`) removes `prShadowVolume` from the options when `CastShadows` is off.

**N. Shadow volume edges paired in linear time.** `TShapeShadowVolumes.CalculateIfNeededManifoldAndBorderEdges` pairs each new edge with the open (border) edges found so far by a linear search, so it is quadratic in the number of open edges: fast for closed meshes (few open edges at a time), very slow for big open meshes (map geometry, props). Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#746](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/746) pairs edges through a hash map keyed by the two vertex positions (linear time, same pairs), with `TTestSceneCore.TestManifoldAndBorderEdgesPairs` and the example `examples/viewport_and_scenes/shadow_volumes_large_open_caster` (a wall of separate tiles: 20 000 triangles 1.81 s -> 0.05 s, 45 000 triangles 10.2 s -> 0.08 s, 180 000 triangles 0.34 s; closed meshes about the same). It matters even for open meshes that never cast: CGE skips shapes with border edges when rendering shadow volumes, but has to calculate the edges to know. F (keeping the pairs across animation) builds on it, and the whole scene manifold detection (`CalculateDetectedWholeSceneManifold`) is quadratic the same way. Workaround: brush entities and static models with no closed surface don't cast (`TrianglesClosed`; CGE skips open shapes when casting anyway), md5 meshes over 1500 triangles cast with boxes per joint.

**O. zlib binding's `z_stream` layout on 64-bit (a CGE bug).** `CastleInternalZLib.TZStream` is declared with `{$PACKRECORDS 4}` and 32-bit `uLong`, so it is 84 bytes on Win64, where C's `z_stream` is 88 (8-byte alignment adds trailing padding), and the layout is wrong on 64-bit Linux / macOS (C's `uLong` is 64-bit there: 112 bytes). zlib checks the size passed to `inflateInit_` / `deflateInit_`, so `inflateInit2` and the other init functions of the binding return `Z_VERSION_ERROR` on 64-bit platforms. CGE itself doesn't call them (its gzip code uses PasZLib), which is why it went unnoticed. Needed: C record packing and `CULong` / `CUInt` / `CLong` types for `uLong`, `uInt` and `z_off_t`. Proposed upstream: [castle-engine/castle-engine#744](https://github.com/castle-engine/castle-engine/pull/744), with `TTestCastleInternalZLib` (the record size, a raw deflate / inflate and a compress / uncompress round trip; on Win64 it fails without the fix: 84 bytes, `Z_VERSION_ERROR`). Workaround: `Doom3Files` passes a padded record (`TPaddedZStream`) on Windows and inflates with FPC's PasZLib elsewhere.

**P. Several environment probes and irradiance volumes.** CGE's `EnvironmentLight` gives image-based lighting from one pair of textures (diffuse and specular) to the shapes it lights. RBDOOM-3-BFG uses one probe per BSP area (blended at area borders) and, for diffuse light, a grid of spherical harmonics per area interpolated per pixel. Needed: a probe chosen per shape (for example an `EnvironmentLight` with a box, used by the shapes inside it), and an irradiance volume (a 3D grid of SH coefficients as a texture, sampled by world position in the physical lighting model). Baking can stay in this port (6 views per probe, convolved on the CPU). Workaround: none (no indirect light, like Doom 3).

**Q. Temporal anti-aliasing.** TAA blends each frame with the previous ones, reprojected by per-pixel motion vectors, with a sub-pixel jitter of the projection every frame. CGE has MSAA and screen effects, but no motion vector output or projection jitter. Workaround: MSAA.

Merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#11](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/11), 2026-10-21): `TCastleViewport.TemporalAntiAliasing`. `ApplyProjection` shifts the projection by a Halton (2, 3) offset of 8 frames; after the world (and the `HighDynamicRange` resolve), before the children and screen effects, `TCastleScreenEffects` blends it with 2 history textures (float when possible): motion vectors from the depth buffer (the nearest depth of the 3x3 neighborhood, so the pixels around a nearer object's edge move with it) and the camera's previous view and projection, the history clamped to the neighborhood's mean +- standard deviation in YCoCg; the result is copied back over the world, keeping depth and stencil. The depth ranges of CGE's layers are decoded (the world in 0.1..1, `rlFront` in 0..0.1, assumed to move with the camera). Ignored with MSAA and in transparent viewports. Tests (edges, jitter, a moving camera without trails, transparent viewports), example `temporal_anti_aliasing`. Not done: per-object motion vectors (moving monsters are only clamped, so they are less anti-aliased), sharpening (the history's bilinear reads soften the image while moving). Here: console `r_taa`.

**R. Shadow map atlas and cascades.** CGE gives each shadow-mapped light its own texture, with a projection fitted to the scene (`AutoCalculateProjectionForDirectionalLight`). RBDOOM-3-BFG packs the shadow maps of all lights (point lights as 6 faces) into one atlas, sized by each light's size on screen, and splits the sun's shadow into cascades by distance. Needed, with B, for many soft-shadowed lights per frame. Workaround: stencil shadow volumes from one light (A for several).

Merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#12](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/12), with B, 2026-10-21): all depth shadow maps are tiles of one depth texture (`ShadowMapsAtlasSize`, 4096; power of 2 squares from a quadtree, halved when they don't fit, kept until the OpenGL context closes), and share one texture unit in the shaders; lookups and PCF are clamped to the tile. `DirectionalLight.shadowCascades` (up to 4) splits the main camera's view by distance, each cascade an orthographic view around its slice's sphere snapped to texels, rendered every frame. Tests, example `shadow_maps_point_lights_and_cascades`. Follow-ups merged into `doom3-port` ([mariuz/castle-engine#13](https://github.com/mariuz/castle-engine/pull/13), 2026-10-23): `ShadowMapsSizeByScreen` chooses the size of spot and point light views by how big their range is on the screen (power of 2, with hysteresis, re-allocated in the atlas); `ShadowMapsCascadeBlend` (0.1) blends the end of each cascade with the next (the last fades out), each cascade covering the blended part of the previous one; `TCastleTransform.CastShadowsWhenHidden` renders invisible (portal-culled) transforms into shadow maps, and `CastShadows = false` now also keeps out of them; the viewport re-renders cascades for its own camera when they were rendered for another one.
